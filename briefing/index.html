The Stonecutter

“When nothing seems to help, I go and look at a stonecutter hammering away at his rock perhaps a hundred times without as much as a crack showing in it. Yet at the hundred and first blow it will split in two — and I know it was not that blow that did it, but all that had gone before.” Jacob Riis

Twelve precedents where decades of illegible, compounding investment preceded a sudden phase change. On each chart, visible progress stays flat while accumulated investment compounds — the shaded wedge between them is value being built that nobody can see yet. Then, at the inflection, the two converge. Pick the objection in the room and the matching precedent answers it.

On the data

Six of the twelve cases carry real, sourced figures (marked ◆): deep learning, the transformer→ChatGPT arc, the World Wide Web, marriage equality, Amazon, and Tesla. Real numbers live in the event markers and the “real numbers” callout — never painted onto the plotted curves, which are a shared 0–100 qualitative index whose job is to render the shape of the dynamic so all twelve stay comparable. The one exception is marriage-equality polling, which is natively 0–100. The remaining six cases are illustrative — drawn to the same compounding-then-inflection shape and never given invented numbers. Every case carries a survivorship caveat by design: these are the rocks that split; others were struck exactly as hard and never cracked.
